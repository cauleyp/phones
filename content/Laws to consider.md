There are some laws that surround this decision. Some are federal, one is state-by-state. This will quickly discuss and link to the laws or movements themselves.

## Federal laws
There are two of them that are based and on the books and must be adhered to regardless if a school is public/private.

### Kari's Law
This [law](https://www.fcc.gov/mlts-911-requirements) states that Mult Lined Telephone Systems (MLTS) must be able to dial 911 without having to get an "outside" line. This law came into effect due to an incident in 2013 a 9 year old girl tried to dial 911 from a hotel room but the call never get and outside line and the result was a man attacked and killed her mother in a hotel room. 

It became law in 2019. 

Since this is a federal law and it is illegal to sell any MTLS without the ability to dial 911 this will be implemented directly.

*Note: Google Voice does not adhere to this because it is not considered an MTLS since the numbers are individual to an account and there is no interoperability between the numbers*

### Ray Baum's Act
This [Act](https://www.fcc.gov/mlts-911-requirements) is also federal and therefore should be included in any system we go with. This Act ensures that when a person dials 911 from a MTLS handset the EMS will have a dispatchable location. So if someone dials 911 from within a building the emergency response team knows where to go or where to be directed to go.

If someone is using their smartphone with an app and dial 911 through the app (not sure why they wouldn't just dial 911), then it will most likely use the phone's GPS to give EMS that location. 

## State laws

### Alyssa's law
This is in response to the death of Alyssa Alhadeff, a 14 year old who died during the Stoneman Douglas High School in Parkland, Florida. The proposed law is not a federal and there is an active movement to get the law passed in all 50 states.

The proposed law would require all buildings to be equipped with a silent panic button that notify local EMS and police. 

*Sycamore currently has a silent panic button that lives up front with Maria*

Since the law is passed state-by-state, the wording and requirements can change. Right now it is passed in only 11 states and proposed on 17 others. Indiana is not included in either of these lists and we should not consider it a mandatory feature that we need to have. 

## E911
This isn't a law, more like an improved 911 service. It stands for "Enhanced 911" This basically means that if someone calls 911 from one of our phones, it will tell the EMTs where they are in the building as opposed to just a 911 call originating from Sycamore School.

You can read more about from the [FCC website](https://www.fcc.gov/general/9-1-1-and-e9-1-1-services).
